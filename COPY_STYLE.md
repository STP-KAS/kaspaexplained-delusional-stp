# Public writing

Write for someone who is curious about Kaspa and may know no cryptocurrency terminology. On builder and specification pages, technical language is appropriate when it adds precision.

Explain a term where it first becomes necessary. Use a concrete payment, block, withdrawal, or quantity instead of abstract language about ecosystems and possibilities. Do not repeat a heading in the next sentence.

Show the lesson in the demo. The surrounding text should explain what the visitor sees, then let them continue. Put specialist detail behind a label that says what is inside. Essential limitations stay visible.

Keep proposals, implementations, releases, activations, tools, and adoption distinct. Date changing claims. A source must support the nearby statement, not merely discuss the same subject.

Avoid uniform paragraph shapes, repeated contrast formulas, decorative slogans, canned transitions, and concluding paragraphs that add no information. Do not introduce mistakes, slang, or forced variation to appear human. There is no word blacklist or density quota.

Use American English and ordinary punctuation. Do not use em dashes. Keep numbers and units together. A short paragraph is welcome when it explains the point; length alone does not measure clarity.

Read the rendered page, including controls, results, metadata, and source notes. Ask what the reader now understands that they did not understand before. Remove text that contributes nothing to that answer.

For X, write a complete social post rather than a heading or isolated proposition. Vary the length with the idea. Keep supporting source lines in the calendar for verification before posting. Do not invent a character limit.

The X posts are separate from the website. They can draw on its research, but must stand alone. Do not add site promotion, demo invitations, or Kaspa Explained links unless requested.

Whenever a daily post is requested, check its changing claims against current primary sources and update it before delivery. The calendar is a draft, not proof of current status. Flag or omit claims that cannot be verified. Keep verification notes outside the copyable post.
